## What $497 is

One main public page, plus the path from first look to first contact (what they see, and how they would call, book, or write).

The buyer receives:

1. A marked-up written review of what helps or hurts the call.
2. A ranked list: **fix now / fix next / leave alone**.
3. Replacement copy for the **highest-impact** section.
4. A short recorded walkthrough of the findings.
5. One written question round for **7 days** after delivery.

Delivery is **5 business days** after intake is complete: owner name, shop name, public page URL, service category, service area, email, phone, and one blocker. Format is a written review plus the ranked list, the replacement copy, and the walkthrough. A live call is not promised.

Price is **$497**, said after the work is clear.

If the packet has **no clear ranked list**, the buyer says so within **7 days of delivery**. Owl redoes it once, or refunds. That is a delivery promise. It is not a promise of calls, jobs, or revenue.

Fulfillment is Alex Anderson, solo. Intake mail is the address already on the door: `owloffersofficial@gmail.com`.

If the page homeowners actually open is a Facebook page, a Google listing, or a one-page site, that URL can be the one page they send. Owl reads it as a visitor would. Owl does not log in.

## FAQ

### What am I paying for?

A human look at one public page and the path to first contact. You get the written review, the ranked list, replacement copy for the highest-impact section, a short recorded walkthrough, and seven days of written questions. Five business days after the intake is complete.

### Is this a social media audit?

People use that phrase. The work is narrower. Owl reads the one page you name, the way a homeowner would, and the path from that page to a call or a note. It is not a score of every profile, and it is not a plan to post more.

### Do you manage Facebook or Instagram ads?

No. Ads management is outside this step. Owl does not spend on Meta from this draft, does not ask for Business Manager access, and does not run ads against the studio door.

### Are you an AI company? Will a bot handle my leads?

No. This is not an AI vendor engagement. Alex reads the page and writes the packet. Nothing in the $497 step installs a chatbot or answers your customers for you.

### I am in Olympia, Tumwater, or Lacey. Do you work with shops here?

Yes, a shop there can send one page. So can a shop somewhere else. This draft is written for that local conversation. The public door does not name the three cities, and Owl does not claim a shopfront or a crew on the ground there.

### My leads are messy. Will this sort them out?

It depends what "messy" is.

If the mess is on the public face — the first screen does not name the job, the number is hard to find, the form does not say what happens next, the listing and the page disagree — that is what the look is for.

If the mess is a pile of old messages, a spreadsheet, or an ad account, this step does not clean it. Say that plainly. Do not take the login to "have a look."

### What do you need from me?

The intake fields: owner, shop, the public page URL, service category, service area, email, phone, and one sentence on what you think is blocking the call. That is enough. Owl does not need a password, an ad account, a customer export, or a card number to start the look.

### How do I pay, and when does the clock start?

The door has no checkout. Intake is an email. The five business days start when intake is complete, not when a stranger first says hello. Do not invent a payment link in the reply. If they ask how to pay, Alex answers that himself, outside this draft.

### What is not in the packet?

Ads, a retainer, extra pages, a promised number of calls, and a second rewrite of a second section. One replacement section is the copy deliverable.

### What if I hate it?

If there is no clear ranked list, say so within seven days of delivery. Owl redoes that packet once, or refunds. Disagreement about taste, or disappointment that the phone did not start ringing, is not the redo trigger. The trigger is a missing ranked list.

### Will you guarantee more jobs?

No. The promise is the packet: review, ranks, one section of replacement copy, a walkthrough, and one question round. Anyone who guarantees a job count from a $497 page look is selling a different product.

### Who does the work?

Alex Anderson. Small studio. The signature on the door is the fulfillment. There is no account team to introduce.

### Can I see an example?

Yes. The SAMPLE on the door is Northfork Fence & Gate. It is fiction, labeled SAMPLE. It shows the shape of the thinking: what the homeowner met, a ranked list, one rewritten section. It is not a client, and it is not a shop in Olympia, Tumwater, or Lacey.

### Do you need to post about my shop?

No. The look is private to the buyer. This draft does not authorize a case study, a before/after on the door, or a social post. Publishing a real shop requires a separate yes from that owner, and it is not part of $497.

### What if my real problem is the phone, not the page?

Say so in the blocker line. The path to first contact includes what a person would do next: the number, the form, the hours, the sentence about a callback. Owl can rank a missing callback window as fix-now. Owl will not staff the phone.

### Can you just fix the page for me?

The $497 packet includes replacement copy for the highest-impact section. It does not include building the page, logging into a website host, or posting the copy live. If they want Owl to keep going after the packet, that is the unpriced stay-on conversation.

## Objections

Each reply stays inside the locked step. The last line of every reply is a stop: do not add a monthly plan, an AI tool, or an ad budget to rescue the close.

### "I don't need an audit. I need more leads."

They heard a document. They are naming thin or messy inquiries.

The $497 step looks at the page a homeowner opens and the path from that look to a call or a note. The packet is a ranked list, replacement copy for the section that matters most, and a short walkthrough. It does not buy leads.

Stop. Do not offer a lead package.

### "So you're going to tell me to post more."

They have heard that from a social manager.

The look does not assign a posting quota. It says what on the named page helps the call, what to fix next, and what to leave alone. Posting more is not a deliverable.

Stop. Do not attach a content calendar.

### "My nephew already runs the ads."

Good. This step does not take the ads over. Owl does not need the ad account. If the nephew's landing page is the page homeowners open, that URL can be the one page. The findings are about that page and the contact path.

Stop. Do not critique their spend, their audiences, or their creative from memory.

### "Is this one of those AI receptionist things?"

No. No bot, no subscription, no software in the packet. A person reads the page and writes what helps or hurts the call.

Stop. Do not demo a tool.

### "I already know the website is bad."

Then the useful part is the rank and the replacement section, not a lecture. Fix now, fix next, leave alone — plus words they can actually put on the highest-impact section. Owners who already know often still do not know what to leave alone.

Stop. Do not pile on extra pages to justify the fee.

### "Can you guarantee I'll book more jobs this month?"

No. Five business days produces a packet. It does not produce a booked month. The only redo-or-refund line is a missing ranked list, raised within seven days of delivery.

Stop. Do not soften this into "results may vary" and then imply results.

### "Why only one page? My whole site is a mess."

Because that is the first step, not the whole firm. One page, one contact path, one rewritten section. If the packet shows a second page matters, stay-on work is a conversation after, and it is not priced here.

Stop. Do not quote a sitewide price.

### "$497 is a lot for a PDF."

Describe the packet before the price: the marked-up review, the ranked list, the replacement copy, the walkthrough, and seven days of written questions, in five business days. The refund line is about delivery of that list, not about leads. Then the price.

Stop. Do not discount in the moment. A discount is a separate yes.

### "$497 is too cheap to be real."

It is cheap relative to a retainer because it is not a retainer. It is one page, one pass, one question round. Ongoing work is a different conversation.

Stop. Do not invent a larger package to sound serious.

### "Send me a proposal with a monthly fee."

The first step has a fixed price and a fixed packet. A monthly fee is ads-management or retainers, and both are outside this step. If they want Owl to stay after the packet, Alex can talk. That talk does not have a rate card on the door.

Stop. Do not draft a monthly proposal in the reply.

### "I don't want to hand over my customers."

You should not. Intake is the page URL and the fields on the form. No customer list, no inbox export, no passwords.

Stop. Do not ask for a screenshot of their DMS "just to see the mess."

### "You're going to use my shop as marketing."

Not from this step. The only public example is a fictional SAMPLE. A real shop stays off the door unless that owner agrees later, in writing, as its own decision.

Stop. Do not promise a feature or a testimonial slot.
